Concept Atlas

How the concept layer works

Concept notes in this vault are not tags. A mature concept note is a reusable analytical synthesis: it explains a mechanism, grounds that mechanism in specific books, distinguishes itself from nearby concepts, and helps compare cases across history, fiction, political economy, technology, war, ecology, religion, and institutions. A book should link to a concept only when the concept materially improves interpretation of that book.

Meta-concepts organize the concept layer. They are not replacements for mature concepts. They explain how families of concepts relate, which book clusters are strongest, and which reading paths make sense.

Redirect and holding notes remain short by design. They keep links alive and route broad terms such as Systems Thinking, Political Economy, or Institutions toward stronger developed concepts.

Mature concept clusters

Order and Governance

Use this cluster when the main question is how authority becomes practical order.

  • State Capacity: Use when the issue is whether an institution can execute: tax, count, build, police, supply, educate, repair, or fight.
  • Crisis Governance: Use when decision-making happens under acute time pressure, fear, uncertainty, or emergency.
  • Polycentric Order: Use when coordination works through multiple centers rather than a single sovereign command.
  • Informal Institutions: Use when norms, patronage, trust, custom, or tacit routines govern actual behavior.
  • Path Dependence and Institutional Drift: Use when inherited forms constrain or redirect later choices.
  • Legitimacy and Succession: Use when authority has to survive death, defeat, transfer, inheritance, abdication, or replacement.

Information, Legibility, and Coordination

Use this cluster when seeing, classifying, transmitting, remembering, or coordinating knowledge is central.

  • Legibility: Use when messy reality is simplified into categories, maps, records, metrics, names, or plans.
  • Information and Coordination: Use when distributed actors need signals, standards, channels, or feedback to act together.
  • Intelligence: Use when knowledge is adversarial, secret, deceptive, and tied to operational decisions.
  • Language and Ideology: Use when vocabulary, slogans, sacred terms, euphemism, or official categories shape perception and justification.
  • Knowledge Preservation: Use when memory, testimony, archives, technical skill, or records survive rupture.
  • Platform Governance: Use when an information layer also governs access, rank, visibility, or conduct.

Economy, Firms, and Infrastructure

Use this cluster for markets, firms, finance, allocation, industrial systems, and the rails that let exchange scale.

  • Commercial Society: Use when exchange reshapes manners, trust, status, obligation, and social order.
  • Illicit Markets: Use when exchange depends on illegality, secrecy, violence, laundering, jurisdictional gaps, or corrupted enforcement.
  • Capital Allocation: Use when surplus, credit, talent, or attention is committed to future capability.
  • Financial Infrastructure: Use when payment, credit, banking, clearing, settlement, or monetary rails are doing the work.
  • Industrial Policy: Use when states coordinate sectors, firms, finance, skills, and national capability.
  • Logistics and Throughput: Use when movement, capacity, bottlenecks, maintenance, and operating flow determine success.
  • Commodity Systems: Use when a key good organizes labor, ecology, logistics, finance, and political leverage.

Empire, Frontier, and Periphery

Use this cluster when power is uneven across centers, margins, routes, colonies, frontiers, or borderlands.

  • Empire and Periphery: Use when central rule is stretched across unequal spaces and depends on local intermediaries.
  • Frontier Systems: Use when weak authority, violence, improvisation, opportunity, and extraction define the setting.
  • Maritime Power: Use when fleets, ports, sea lanes, naval protection, and oceanic logistics decide outcomes.
  • Chokepoints and Gateways: Use when disproportionate leverage comes from controlling a mandatory route, passage, interface, or access layer.
  • Coercive Labor: Use when work is extracted through slavery, bondage, confinement, corvee, dependency, or structural force.
  • Commodity Systems: Use here when imperial power is organized around goods such as silver, opium, rubber, sugar, bison robes, or spice.

War, Crisis, and Collapse

Use this cluster for violence, emergency, breakdown, survival, adaptation, and recovery.

  • Collapse and Resilience: Use when systems fail, absorb shocks, rebuild, or preserve continuity under stress.
  • Antifragility and Optionality: Use when slack, modularity, exit, redundancy, or bounded downside preserves adaptive room.
  • Crisis Governance: Use here when emergency rule, triage, or command under stress is central.
  • State Capacity: Use here when war or disaster tests the execution ability of institutions.
  • Information and Coordination: Use here when crisis turns on signals, command, trust, or communication failure.

Culture, Identity, and Legitimacy

Use this cluster for belonging, sacred authority, elite reproduction, moral obligation, and the stories that make rule plausible.

  • National Identity: Use when populations become imagined as nations, peoples, or historical communities.
  • Elite Formation: Use when ruling, expert, clerical, military, or professional groups are selected and reproduced.
  • Religion and Legitimacy: Use when sacred or civil-religious authority authorizes order.
  • Legitimacy and Succession: Use here when continuity of authority depends on ritual, inheritance, marriage, education, or symbolic transfer.
  • Moral Economy: Use when fairness, reciprocity, subsistence, protection, or rightful exchange is the issue.
  • Language and Ideology: Use here when identity is built through vocabulary, narrative, slogans, or official categories.

Technology, Platforms, and Speculative Systems

Use this cluster for technical systems, platforms, science fiction, artificial intelligence, and institutions under altered technological conditions.

  • Technological Change: Use when invention, diffusion, complements, maintenance, lock-in, and institutional absorption are central.
  • Artificial Intelligence: Use when technical artifacts interpret, decide, teach, deceive, or act as delegated agents.
  • Platform Governance: Use when mediation layers govern users through access, identity, data, ranking, defaults, or enforcement.
  • Speculative Governance: Use when fiction is being used as an institutional laboratory.
  • Information and Coordination: Use here when technology changes how signals or standards coordinate action.
  • Chokepoints and Gateways: Use here when technical control concentrates at interfaces, protocols, payment rails, farcasters, or cloud layers.

Ecology, Resources, and Constraint

Use this cluster when material environments, resources, energy, disease, or biological limits shape institutional possibility.

  • Ecological Constraint: Use when environmental limits and feedback define what institutions can do.
  • Commodity Systems: Use here when resource flows drive political or ecological change.
  • Frontier Systems: Use here when settlement or extraction pushes into difficult environments.
  • Collapse and Resilience: Use here when ecological stress produces breakdown or adaptation.
  • Logistics and Throughput: Use here when resources depend on movement, storage, energy, and repair.

Memory, Narrative, and Knowledge Preservation

Use this cluster when testimony, records, archives, oral memory, technical continuity, or cultural inheritance are central.

  • Knowledge Preservation: Use when memory, skill, records, and testimony survive disruption.
  • Language and Ideology: Use when vocabulary makes memory usable or distorts it.
  • National Identity: Use when memory becomes collective belonging.
  • Religion and Legitimacy: Use when sacred memory authorizes rule or endurance.
  • Information and Coordination: Use when memory has to become actionable coordination.

Choosing between nearby concepts

Use State Capacity for ability to execute; use Legitimacy and Succession for why authority is accepted. Napoleon's Conseil d'Etat and Bank of France are capacity; his coronation, dynasty, and St Helena legend are legitimacy.

Use Legibility for simplification by institutions; use Language and Ideology for the words that shape perception and justification. Mann's caste labels belong to both, but legibility asks how they classify people, while language and ideology asks what those labels make morally and politically thinkable.

Use Information and Coordination for signals, standards, and shared channels; use Knowledge Preservation for durable memory, testimony, and skill. Visa authorization messages coordinate; Nahua petitions and Levi's testimony preserve.

Use Empire and Periphery for center-edge rule; use Frontier Systems for contested zones where authority is weak and improvisation is high. Canton is an imperial gateway; Lakota river valleys and Fort Laramie are frontier systems with their own power.

Use Maritime Power for sea control and naval logistics; use Chokepoints and Gateways for any mandatory passage layer, maritime or not. The Cape route and Dutch fleets are maritime power; Canton, Visa, and farcasters are gateways.

Use Commodity Systems for a good's full institutional world; use Commercial Society for how exchange reshapes social relations. Rubber plantations and Potosi silver are commodity systems; merchant manners and contract trust belong to commercial society.

Use Collapse and Resilience for survival and recovery; use Antifragility and Optionality for preserved upside, exit, slack, and adaptive variation. Postwar reconstruction is resilience; Europe's fragmented rival patrons in Escape from Rome are optionality.

Use Technological Change for invention and diffusion; use Platform Governance when a technical layer controls participation. Stillsuits are technological change; the Metaverse, Visa, the Feed, and farcaster Web are platform governance when they set terms of access.

Use Artificial Intelligence when a machine or formal procedure acts as delegated judgment; use Information and Coordination when the main issue is signals, standards, or shared channels. The Daemon, Aineko, Speedy, and the Primer are AI cases; Room 40's message flow and Visa authorization are coordination cases unless automated judgment is central.

Use Illicit Markets when forbidden exchange depends on secrecy, violence, laundering, or jurisdictional gaps; use Commodity Systems when the good's full labor, ecological, financial, and logistical world is primary. Canton opium can require both, but Lintin receiving ships and smuggling enforcement point to illicit markets while opium's production and silver effects point to commodity systems.

Use Intelligence when knowledge is collected or interpreted against an adversary; use Knowledge Preservation when the main issue is durable memory, testimony, or archived skill. Smiley and Room 40 are intelligence; Levi's camp testimony and Nahua records are knowledge preservation.

Use Religion and Legitimacy for sacred authorization; use Moral Economy for expectations of fairness and reciprocity. Fremen prophecy is religion and legitimacy; peasant subsistence guarantees and betrayed protection are moral economy.

Redirect and holding notes

These notes are intentionally short because the currently inspected book support is either too narrow or better absorbed by mature concepts.

  • Systems Thinking routes to Information and Coordination, Collapse and Resilience, and Path Dependence and Institutional Drift.
  • Information Theory routes to Information and Coordination unless a book is specifically about signals, noise, encoding, computation, or formal information.
  • Political Economy routes to Commercial Society, Capital Allocation, Industrial Policy, Financial Infrastructure, and State Capacity.
  • Institutions routes to State Capacity, Informal Institutions, Path Dependence and Institutional Drift, and Legitimacy and Succession.
  • Legitimacy routes to Religion and Legitimacy or Legitimacy and Succession.

Maintenance

Prefer strengthening existing concepts over creating new ones. Create a new mature concept only when several inspected book notes support it and when it cannot be absorbed cleanly by an existing mature concept.

Every related-concept link should explain the relationship. Every book example should name concrete people, institutions, places, events, mechanisms, or cases from the book.

When concept overlap is real, do not force artificial separation. Use the nearest concept for the main mechanism and link adjacent concepts in the explanation.
