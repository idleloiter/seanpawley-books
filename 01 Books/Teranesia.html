Detailed overview

Greg Egan builds Teranesia around two linked returns: a return to the island where Prabir Suresh's parents died, and a return by DNA to biological possibilities that should have remained inaccessible. The novel opens on a nameless rock in Maluku propinsi that Prabir calls Teranesia, a private childhood kingdom in the Banda Sea near the Kai and Tanimbar Islands. His parents, Rajendra and Radha, are Indian biologists from Calcutta studying a strange green-and-black swallowtail butterfly whose wing veins, genital openings, developmental hormones, and DNA markers make ordinary taxonomy fail. Prabir, his baby sister Madhusree, Silk Rainbow's sponsorship, and the island's isolation are established before the Indonesian Emergency turns scientific fieldwork into a war-zone story.

The family history is as important as the evolutionary puzzle. Rajendra's atheism and biology are traced back to an Indian Rationalists Association demonstration in which a snakebite holy man fails to save a dog; Radha enters through karate classes for Scheduled Caste women, genetics at the University of Calcutta, and a break with her "upper-middle-class pseudo-socialist" parents. Egan uses those memories to show why Prabir inherits not just scientific habits but a moral posture: truth should be tested against superstition, bodies are material systems, and courage is not the same as faith. The later scenes with hooks, cheek skewers, and the Calcutta truck-pulling parade keep returning as Prabir's private standard for what his parents taught him.

The early disaster is political, accidental, and psychological at once. Prabir paints ILMUWAN on the huts to mark them as scientists, hears jets during the Emergency, hides his internet friendship with Eleanor in New York behind his father's identity, and sends a message that makes Rajendra sound sympathetic to the Republik Maluku Selatan. After mines kill Radha and Rajendra in the garden, ABRMS guerrillas dump Prabir and Madhusree on Yamdena, a Chinese woman from eastern Java gets them onto a boat south, and they reach the detention camp near Exmouth before being sent to Keith and Amita in Toronto. Prabir's adult life is shaped by the conviction that he saved Madhusree and killed their parents.

Toronto is not a pause from the main plot; it is where Egan shows the cost of survival. Prabir grows up with Amita's academic performance politics, Keith's hostility, And Poets, Oliver Stone's Ramanujan biopic, Madhusree's questions about Radha and Rajendra, and his own delayed sexuality. His relationship with Felix, whose artificial retinas feed a tactile electrode sheet on his back, gives the novel one of its central biological conversations: Prabir wants love and sex to be stolen from evolution's "great dumb machine," while Felix proposes an "oxbow lake" model in which infertile adults may help kin. That conversation is not decoration; it is the human version of the novel's later question about whether life ever serves anything but replication.

When strange mutants appear across the South Moluccas, Madhusree joins a biological expedition and Prabir follows her. The new fieldwork passes through Ambon, Martha Grant's magnetohydrodynamic boat, Banda Sea navigation, Bandanaira's abandoned nutmeg plantations, mutant fruit pigeons, tree frogs, bats, barbed-wire shrubs, Teranesia's ruined huts, Seli Ojany, a giant python in the mangroves, and scientists posting data to the net. Egan contrasts serious biology with opportunistic spectacle through Paul Sutton's "ecotropy," Cole's feverish rhetoric, tourist entrepreneurs pitching animatronic dinosaurs, and competing university and pharmaceutical incentives. The source of wonder is also a source of commerce, career ambition, territorial fear, and danger.

The final mechanism is the Sao Paulo protein, a gene product that appears to copy useful counterfactual genetic alternatives from other quantum histories. Joaquim Furtado's theory, PharmoNucleic, Lausanne's synthetic chromosome test, Grant's RNA analyses, dormant butterflies, endonucleases, liposomes, diapause, and parasitic transmission turn Egan's biological speculation into a brutal final test when Prabir becomes infected. Madhusree defeats the gene not by destroying it, but by offering infected cells an easier reproductive route in culture flasks, pickle jars, growth medium, and scavenged glassware on the way to Darwin. The ending's line that "Life is meaningless" is not nihilistic filler; it is the practical insight that lets Madhusree trick a system that wants nothing but more of itself.

Core concepts and linkages

  • Ecological Constraint: Island geography, mutant ecologies, parasites, pythons, mines, mangroves, and spreading biological change make environment an active constraint on science and survival.
  • Technological Change: The Sao Paulo gene behaves like a biological search technology, changing what mutation, adaptation, and intervention can mean.
  • Knowledge Preservation: Prabir's memories, recovered butterfly specimens, bar codes, public data releases, and Radha and Rajendra's restored credit turn family trauma into scientific record.
  • Crisis Governance: The Indonesian Emergency, Exmouth detention, militia quarantine panic, WHO delays, and improvised Darwin evacuation force decisions under uncertainty.
  • Empire and Periphery: Maluku, West Papua, Jakarta, Australian detention, Toronto, pharmaceuticals, and field expeditions show peripheral islands pulled into state, corporate, and scientific power.
  • Information and Coordination: Net messages, posted data, translation software, public sequence databases, and counterfactual genetic information determine what actors can know in time to act.

Chapter-by-chapter notes

Chapter 1

Summary: Prabir names the island Teranesia after finding it absent from Dutch and Portuguese charts, local Kai and Tanimbar naming practice, and Indonesian maps of Maluku propinsi. He plays with fifteen-month-old Madhusree on the beach, calculates the horizon from the Earth's radius, imagines climbing the volcanic cone to verify the distance to the Tanimbar Islands, rescues Madhusree from swimming too far by inventing a water man on the reef, and returns her to Radha in the butterfly hut. The chapter introduces Rajendra's Latin butterfly naming, Prabir's mosquito-repellent implant, the Banda Sea, the kampung of prefabricated huts, fruit pigeons, nutmeg trees, and the forbidden scientific space where the butterflies live. Source anchors: Teranesia; Madhusree; water man; butterfly hut; Banda Sea; mosquito repellent.

Analysis: Teranesia begins as Prabir's act of naming, which makes the island personal before it becomes scientific evidence and trauma site. Madhusree and water man establish his protective method: he guides danger through stories instead of direct force, a habit that later becomes both tenderness and deception. Butterfly hut and mosquito repellent separate Prabir from his parents' research, giving him a child's resentment and fascination toward the very specimens that later carry Radha and Rajendra's unfinished work.

Chapter 2

Summary: The Indonesian Emergency enters Teranesia through net blackouts, BBC shortwave, ABRMS, Aceh, Irian Jaya, Jakarta, Ambon, and military aircraft. Radha and Rajendra decide to paint ILMUWAN on every hut, and Prabir helps mark the photovoltaic roofs and walls while managing Madhusree's tantrum and his mother's fear. At night, Prabir sneaks into the butterfly hut, remembers Rajendra's microscope in Calcutta, reaches into a cage of pupae, hears a low aircraft pass overhead, and wipes his mosquito-repellent sweat onto a chrysalis as a private act of sabotage. Source anchors: ILMUWAN; ABRMS; photovoltaic roofs; chrysalis; low aircraft; Calcutta microscope.

Analysis: ILMUWAN is the family's attempt to make scientific neutrality visible to soldiers, while ABRMS and low aircraft show how fragile that protection is once war reads signs through suspicion. The chrysalis scene turns Prabir's political anxiety into a biological crime against Rajendra's work; he does not yet understand why he feels guilty, but the act binds butterfly research, the Emergency, and his fear of exposure. Calcutta microscope makes the betrayal intimate rather than random, and the chapter is Crisis Governance from a child's scale: a family tries to signal neutrality inside an information environment where every sign can be misread.

Chapter 3

Summary: This chapter supplies Radha and Rajendra's backstory through Prabir's later reconstruction for Eleanor. Rajendra chooses biology after an Indian Rationalists Association demonstration in which a snakebite holy man fails to save a dog before a thousand witnesses; Radha appears as a karate instructor for Scheduled Caste women, a genetics student at the University of Calcutta, and the daughter of parents Prabir calls upper-middle-class pseudo-socialist hypocrites. The chapter follows their courtship, Rajendra's scholarship, Radha leaving home, an attack on Rajendra, IRA parades with hooks and cheek skewers, a Swedish collector's strange swallowtail, Ambon dealers, Kai Besar fishermen, Silk Rainbow's funding, and the move to the island of butterflies. Source anchors: Indian Rationalists Association; Radha; Rajendra; Swedish collector; Silk Rainbow; Kai Besar.

Analysis: Indian Rationalists Association explains why the novel treats bodies, pain, and belief with hard materialism rather than mystical awe. Radha and Rajendra's courtship makes biology a shared vocation, not only a profession, so the Swedish collector and Silk Rainbow do more than launch a plot: they bring a family ideal to Teranesia. Kai Besar connects the butterfly's mystery to local routes and informal knowledge, foreshadowing the later dependence on fishermen, dealers, and guides, which is why Knowledge Preservation in the book depends on both laboratory record and oral route knowledge.

Chapter 4

Summary: Prabir receives Eleanor's warning from New York about Kopasus troops, ABRMS bases, Ambon, Tual, provincial austerity, Jakarta's exploitation of timber, fishing, and mineral rights, and the danger of remaining on the island. To reassure her, he dictates a message while pretending to be Rajendra, praising the end of the Javanese empire and signing off with "Long live the Republik Maluku Selatan." Soon afterward, while carrying Madhusree back from the beach, he hears an explosion from the kampung; mines have killed Radha and Rajendra, and soldiers or guerrillas force the children into flight through Yamdena, the Tanimbar Islands, a Chinese woman from eastern Java, a boat south, and the Exmouth detention camp. Source anchors: Eleanor; Kopasus; Republik Maluku Selatan; mines; Yamdena; Exmouth.

Analysis: Eleanor and Kopasus make Prabir's childish secrecy consequential because adult politics enters through a channel he thinks he can manage. Republik Maluku Selatan is the phrase that later condemns him in his own mind, even though the mines could have been dropped for many reasons. Yamdena and Exmouth shift his role from imaginative brother to sole guardian; the deaths of Radha and Rajendra become inseparable from his responsibility for Madhusree's survival.

Chapter 5

Summary: The children arrive in Canada after the detention camp, where Keith and Amita become foster parents in Toronto. Prabir endures Amita's dinner-party circle of lecturers, sculptors, dentists, actors, architects, accountants, and poets, the And Poets' Ramanujan comparisons, an Oliver Stone biopic full of Hindu deities and infinity snakes, footage from Aceh and Irian Jaya, and Amita's anti-racism speech claiming women were in Belsen, Dachau, and Auschwitz. Keith threatens institutions after Prabir asks to see Amita's camp tattoo, and Prabir later tells Madhusree about Radha and Rajendra through photographs, cheek skewers, truck-pulling, Calcutta, and Bengali memory. Source anchors: Toronto; Amita; And Poets; Ramanujan; Auschwitz; truck-pulling.

Analysis: Toronto and Amita show that rescue is not the same as belonging; Prabir is safe but surrounded by adults whose symbolic politics feel predatory to him. Ramanujan and Auschwitz explain his contempt for people who turn inherited suffering or culture into performance. Truck-pulling restores Radha and Rajendra as embodied models of courage and rationalism, and it gives Madhusree access to parents she cannot remember except through Prabir.

Chapter 6

Summary: As an adult, Prabir writes credit-card investment-adviser software for a Toronto bank, lives with Madhusree, and dates Felix, a Royal Ontario Museum preservationist whose malformed retinas are connected to a tactile electrode sheet on his back. The chapter moves through Bay Street, anthropomorphic financial plug-ins, Felix's eighteenth-century coins, Madhusree's cladistics test, Prabir's fear of custody scrutiny, gay celibacy while raising his sister, Felix's visual cortex massage, the oxbow lake analogy for homosexuality, Prabir's desire to "steal" pleasure from reproduction, and Madhusree's request for five thousand dollars to join a South Moluccas field expedition investigating mutant fruit pigeons, tree frogs, and bats. Source anchors: Felix; electrode sheet; oxbow lake; Madhusree; cladistics; South Moluccas.

Analysis: Felix and electrode sheet make perception and embodiment intimate before the novel returns to mutation and altered bodies. Oxbow lake gives Prabir a biological account of his own life that wounds him because it sounds like his guardianship of Madhusree has been programmed by kin selection. South Moluccas and cladistics bring the adult plot back to the parents' butterfly research, forcing Prabir to choose between Madhusree's scientific autonomy and his own fear.

Chapter 7

Summary: Prabir follows Madhusree to Ambon after lying that he cannot afford her expedition fee. He meets Martha Grant after a mutant bird with unusual saliva attacks her in a bar, learns that she has a magnetohydrodynamic boat and no guide, and offers himself by claiming to have grown up in the islands. Their conversation covers Ambon harbour, Grant's Cardiff family, her pharmaceuticals sponsor, translation software, his hidden sister search, the weather metaphor for danger, and his inability to explain why Madhusree refuses contact. Source anchors: Ambon; Martha Grant; mutant bird; magnetohydrodynamic boat; Cardiff; pharmaceuticals sponsor.

Analysis: Ambon and Martha Grant give Prabir a route back to the archipelago without admitting the truth about Teranesia. The mutant bird proves that the butterfly problem has spread beyond his childhood island, while the magnetohydrodynamic boat offers a technological contrast to the ferries and refugee boats of his past. Cardiff and pharmaceuticals sponsor make Grant neither academic idealist nor corporate villain; she is a practical scientist with her own constraints.

Chapter 8

Summary: Prabir and Grant depart Ambon on the MHD boat, discuss fuel cells, polymer engines, superconducting coils, eel swimming, evolution's wastefulness, and technology's capacity to refine natural tricks. They sail through the Banda Sea to Gunung Api, Bandanaira, Lontar, abandoned nutmeg plantations, Dutch fort remains, kanari almond trees, rattan, lianas, fruit pigeons, and a borrowed camera trap. Prabir spars with Paul Sutton's cosmic "ecotropy" ideas, hears titles like The Genesis Gene and Gaia's Bastards, and presses Grant on mutagenic toxins, Ceram tree frogs, viruses, and the possibility that whatever helps the animals also helps its own spread. Source anchors: MHD boat; Gunung Api; Bandanaira; fruit pigeons; Paul Sutton; ecotropy.

Analysis: MHD boat and Gunung Api put Prabir physically back into the geography he has avoided while letting Egan stage an argument between engineered elegance and evolutionary accident, and the chapter is a case for Ecological Constraint because the mutant ecology is a material limit that reshapes what survival and study require. Fruit pigeons connect the childhood bird life of Teranesia to the new mutant ecology, so the constraint is not a single species but a spreading change in what the islands can grow and what humans can predict about them. Paul Sutton and ecotropy are comic but precise counters to Grant and Prabir: spectacle and metaphysical pattern-seeking crowd around discoveries before the evidence is understood, which is the ecological-constraint warning that the environment changes faster than the categories brought to describe it.

Chapter 9

Summary: On the island of the mangroves, Prabir and Grant search through reef, swamp, barbed-wire shrubs, mutant animals, and dangerous terrain before becoming separated. Prabir is seized by a giant python in the mangroves, loses his knife, almost drowns, and is saved by Seli Ojany, a biologist from Madhusree's expedition who beats the snake with a branch. The scene introduces Ojany, wild pigs, salt-water crocodiles, the expedition's proximity, Grant's return from the jungle, and Ojany's amused recognition that Prabir is Madhusree's brother who followed her all the way from Toronto. Source anchors: mangroves; giant python; Seli Ojany; wild pigs; salt-water crocodiles; Toronto.

Analysis: Mangroves and giant python literalize Prabir's childhood water-man stories as an adult encounter that no story can control. Seli Ojany rescues him, reversing his fantasy that only he can protect others and placing him inside Madhusree's scientific world as the vulnerable one. Toronto returns in Ojany's recognition, turning his secret pursuit into comic exposure and preparing the confrontation with Madhusree.

Chapter 10

Summary: Prabir reaches the expedition camp, where tents, hurricane lamps, postdocs, barflies from Ambon, Paul Sutton, Cole, Mike Carpenter, and biologists arguing about transposons surround Madhusree's team. He meets Madhusree, Deborah, and Laila, learns that the expedition is posting behavioural, anatomical, and DNA data to the net, and receives Madhusree's cold suggestion that Felix could have followed everything from Toronto. At two in the morning, Madhusree wakes him and attacks him for following her, refusing to trust her judgement, and forcing her to reach Radha and Rajendra through his memories; she says she will disclose the butterflies and recover their remains from Teranesia for proper ceremonies. Source anchors: expedition camp; Deborah; Laila; transposons; Felix; proper ceremonies.

Analysis: Expedition camp strips Prabir of special knowledge because Madhusree's colleagues already have data, methods, and danger protocols. Felix becomes her rebuke: distance would not have prevented love or knowledge if Prabir had trusted her. Proper ceremonies wounds him because it challenges his eighteen-year avoidance of Radha and Rajendra's bodies and makes Madhusree's grief independent from the memories he has curated for her.

Chapter 11

Summary: Prabir recruits Grant in secret by revealing that Radha and Rajendra were biologists, that they studied the first mutant on Teranesia, and that Silk Rainbow buried their work. He demands that Grant and PharmoNucleic release all their data publicly before he takes her to the source; after dawn conversations with Subhi, a Muslim fisherman from the Kai Islands whose Christian pela community raised him after his village was destroyed, Grant shows that PharmoNucleic has posted the data with its plasmid logo. Prabir and Grant return to Teranesia, discuss the Suresh butterflies, find the old island on Grant's chart, pass the reef, see the volcanic cone, enter the ruined kampung, kill a python in the butterfly hut, and recover twelve preserved specimens with bar codes. Source anchors: PharmoNucleic; Subhi; pela; Teranesia; preserved specimens; bar codes.

Analysis: PharmoNucleic and Subhi set two forms of responsibility beside each other: corporate control can be redirected toward public data, while pela gives Subhi a non-state ethic that saved him after war. Teranesia and preserved specimens let Prabir finally convert family memory into evidence that credits Radha and Rajendra. Bar codes make the recovery unsentimental and scientific even while he breaks down in the ruined butterfly hut, turning family grief into Information and Coordination that other scientists can verify.

Chapter 12

Summary: After finding the specimens, Prabir tries to send Grant back to the boat so he can enter the mined garden and die where Radha and Rajendra died. The mine detector warns of a mine at seventeen metres, Grant hides nearby with tranquilliser darts and a rifle, and Prabir confesses the Eleanor message, the fake Rajendra identity, the Republik Maluku Selatan phrase, the low aircraft, the poisoned chrysalis, and his belief that he killed his parents. Grant wounds his leg to stop him, then helps him fence the mined garden with branches, warning signs in six languages, a chainsaw, and a mallet. Source anchors: mine detector; Eleanor message; poisoned chrysalis; Grant; warning signs; mined garden.

Analysis: Mine detector and mined garden force Prabir's guilt into the open as geography rather than memory: he can point to the place where he thinks the chain began and ended. Eleanor message and poisoned chrysalis reveal that his whole adult protectiveness has been tangled with concealment. Grant and warning signs do not absolve him; they make him perform one concrete act of care that preserves life instead of trying to erase pain by dying.

Chapter 13

Summary: Grant sequences tissue from the preserved butterflies and finds that the Sao Paulo gene changed over twenty years, refining the protein that rewrites parts of the genome. She posts the historical data crediting Radha and Rajendra Suresh, analyses RNA in dormant adults, and discusses diapause-like peptides, endonucleases, liposomes, parasitic larvae, and the possibility that the gene spreads by moving through predators such as fruit pigeons. Prabir and Grant then receive Joaquim Furtado's theory from Sao Paulo: the protein may exploit quantum states, counterfactual cousins, Hilbert space, entropy minima, and quantum parallelism to copy successful alternatives from other histories. Source anchors: Sao Paulo gene; Radha and Rajendra; diapause; endonuclease; Joaquim Furtado; counterfactual cousins.

Analysis: Sao Paulo gene turns the butterfly mystery into a recursive mechanism, and the chapter is a case for Technological Change because the mutator is a biological capability that reshapes what evolution itself can do. The mutator has improved its own ability to mutate, so a gene product becomes a tool that searches design space far faster than ordinary selection, which is the technological-change pattern of a capability crossing from nature into an instrument that humans must understand and govern. Radha and Rajendra receive scientific credit at last, making the recovered specimens more than relics. Joaquim Furtado and counterfactual cousins radicalize Grant’s biological hypothesis by making the useful mutations come from tested alternatives in quantum histories, which gives the gene a terrifyingly effective search method and turns evolution’s blind machine into something closer to an engineered search.

Chapter 14

Summary: Returning to the expedition, Prabir and Grant are captured by Colonel Aslan and West Papua's Lord's Army militia, whose soldiers wear camouflage trousers, combat boots, Chicago Bulls and Dallas Cowboys shirts, and born-again Christian certainties. Aslan interrogates Grant about contagion, World Health Organisation delays, and mutant threats, then questions Prabir about sex, Islam, homosexuality, and whether he raped Grant. The militia's fear of spreading abominations escalates into a forced evacuation; Prabir, Madhusree, Grant, Ojany, the expedition, boats, dinghies, and infected exposure become entangled as the biological problem becomes political violence again. Source anchors: Colonel Aslan; Lord's Army; West Papua; contagion; homosexuality; World Health Organisation.

Analysis: Colonel Aslan and Lord's Army repeat the novel's earlier Emergency in a new form: armed men turn scientific uncertainty into moral panic. Contagion and World Health Organisation show why the mutants cannot remain a lab problem once borders and agriculture are imagined to be at risk. Homosexuality lets Prabir answer Aslan's sexual suspicion with a truth he once hid, marking how far he has moved from Toronto's fear and secrecy. The chapter is Empire and Periphery because peripheral field science is suddenly governed by militia theology, border anxiety, and distant international health institutions.

Chapter 15

Summary: The final chapter follows Prabir's infection as plaques and carapace-like tissue immobilize him in the dinghy while Madhusree tries growth factor blockers, antisense DNA, tranquilliser, blood sampling, and tissue tests. Lausanne reports that Furtado's synthetic chromosome was randomized, leaving the counterfactual theory standing; Prabir concludes the Sao Paulo gene is making a human chrysalis and asks Madhusree to kill and burn him. Tranquilliser fails, diesel blisters the carapace, and Madhusree realizes the gene wants replication, not Prabir; she draws grey fluid from a boil, grows infected cells in culture flasks and scavenged glassware, offers the gene an easier reproductive peak, reaches Darwin harbour, and hears Prabir promise Calcutta and truck-pulling next year. Source anchors: plaques; antisense DNA; Lausanne; human chrysalis; culture flasks; Darwin harbour.

Analysis: Plaques and human chrysalis make the Sao Paulo gene's logic intimate: counterfactual biology is no longer a marvel in pigeons or butterflies but an assault on Prabir's personhood. Lausanne confirms that Madhusree cannot rely on normal treatment because the gene can answer many interventions by searching nearby biological possibilities. Culture flasks and Darwin harbour deliver the book's solution: Madhusree wins by understanding that life is meaningless in the specific sense that replication can be diverted from everything humans value, a final application of Information and Coordination to biological search space.
