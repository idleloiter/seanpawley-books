Empire Games

Position in the vault

Book 1 of the Empire Games series, a continuity sequel to the original Merchant Princes arc. The local series hub is Empire Games; the sibling volume Dark State continues the Commonwealth–US contact begun here, and the series hub records that the concluding volume Invisible Sun is not locally represented. Routed against the earlier work through Merchant Princes, which covers the six original novels in which the Clan, Miriam Beckstein, and the world-walking trade are established.

Where the Merchant Princes volumes framed world-walking as dynastic smuggling and state-formation pressure, Empire Games reframes the same resource as rival para-time state infrastructures. It sits thematically beside the intelligence-and-security reading path, Intelligence, Warning, and Institutional Self-Deception, and the governance paths Speculative Governance and Future Orders and State Formation, Legibility, and Informal Order, and feeds the maps Science Fiction and Speculative Orders and State, Order, and Legibility.

Detailed overview

Charles Stross opens Empire Games by making a family secret into a national security instrument. Rita Douglas begins as a broke Boston actor working HaptoTech motion-capture demos in Seattle, but the Department of Homeland Security already knows she is Miriam Beckstein's daughter, an inactive JAUNT BLUE carrier, and the older outlier in the Clan's DRAGON'S TEETH breeding records. Colonel Eric Smith, Dr. Scranton, Sonia Gomez, and Patrick O'Neill do not recruit her because she wants adventure; they corner her with a false-flag kidnapping, a rooted phone, a job offer she cannot safely refuse, and the promise that they can turn her into a world-walker.

The American chapters are built around the difference between formal legality and actual coercion. Rita is never quite under arrest, yet she is held in warehouse rooms, hangars, Camp Graceland, clinics, and windowless offices where consent has little practical meaning. The United States of time line two has para-time machines, genome scanners, ARMBAND devices, drones, street cameras, and federal databases, but it has forgotten old human tradecraft. Kurt Douglas, Greta, Angie Hagen, and the Wolf Orchestra preserve that older craft through dead drops, handwritten letters, one-time pads, stripped RFID tags, and Stasi habits that the modern panopticon can miss.

The Commonwealth plot gives the other side equal weight. Miriam Burgeson, now a People's Commissioner in the New American Commonwealth, has spent seventeen years trying to make time line three technologically self-sufficient before the United States arrives. Her prison-camp proposal to Adam Burroughs becomes microprocessor factories, Dawn One, high-speed rail, nuclear submarines, and the Department of Para-historical Research. But progress brings fear: John Frederick and the Dauphin see the Commonwealth's technology as a royalist emergency, Adrian Holmes sees the Burgesons as rivals, and Air Defense Command's shootdowns of American drones push the Commonwealth toward nuclear alert.

Rita's BLACK RAIN missions join the espionage plot to the geopolitical one. Her first switchyard operation finds English lettering, five-foot track gauge, overhead electric freight, blue signal lamps, and Irongate Imports. Her second sees a four o'clock shift change, computer terminals, electric multiple units, broad public transport, planned housing clues, and flatcars carrying tanks. These details force Smith and Scranton to treat BLACK RAIN not as a primitive unknown but as an industrial, nuclear-capable cousin with enough competence to shoot down drones and perhaps enough knowledge to catch world-walkers.

The novel's emotional hinge is Rita's divided loyalty. Smith needs her to love the Unit, fear BLACK RAIN, and trust the United States enough not to defect; Kurt knows that secret police always lie; Angie reappears as lover, electrical contractor, and third-generation Orchestra contact; Miriam is both birth mother and foreign minister. When Inspector Alice Morgan captures Rita in Irongate and Olga Thorold reveals that Miriam has known of Rita for only an hour, the diplomatic channel begins as a wound. The book ends with Miriam's letter, a DNA swab, more than nine thousand Commonwealth hydrogen bombs, Adam Burroughs's terminal cancer, and Kurt raising the Wolf Orchestra because DHS has taken his granddaughter.

Structurally the novel alternates grounded third-person scenes with classified voice transcripts, each flagged by location and date and closed with END TRANSCRIPT. That alternating form makes the individual chapters exercises in institutional information control. The reader overhears the planning conversations the Americans never show Rita and the threat assessments the Commonwealth never publishes, so both intelligence cultures are exposed as organizations that consume and meter information rather than simply gather it. The diversionary mission transcripts Scranton mandates in the inner cell complete the pattern: within the American side, the recorded word is no longer even what the operation claims.

The afterword reframes the plot as a future historical extract by Bruce Schneier, making the Office of Special Programs and the Family Trade Organization part of a bureaucratic history after 7/16. That history matters because it explains how an agency with fewer than four hundred staff became DHS's para-time CIA, how oil and carbon dumping reshaped transportation security, and why the origin of world-walking nanotechnology points beyond the Clan to an unknown advanced civilization.

Primary characters and motivations

  • Rita Douglas: a twenty-five-year-old actor and JAUNT BLUE carrier, adopted at birth by Emily and Franz Douglas. Motivations: keep her family safe, retain some control over her own life, and understand the orphaned identity DHS and the Clan both claim. Her arc moves from coerced recruit, to sent reconnaissance asset, to captured messenger and blood relative of a foreign minister. Meeting Angie Hagen and reconnecting with Kurt give her emotional anchors the Unit cannot police.
  • Miriam Burgeson (formerly Beckstein): Rita's birth mother and the Commonwealth Commissioner for the Ministry of Intertemporal Technological Intelligence. Motivations: immunize the Commonwealth against the United States through accelerated technological self-sufficiency, survive the revolutionary succession after Adam Burroughs, and now reconcile with the daughter she gave up in 1994. Her prison-camp pitch is the seed of the entire Commonwealth modernization program.
  • Colonel Eric Smith: retired USAF officer, NSA alum, and founder of the Unit, the human-intelligence arm of the Office of Special Programs. Motivations: reconstitute old-style HUMINT tradecraft against BLACK RAIN, protect the nation from para-time threats, and keep his "personal project" (the Unit) from being buried by bureaucratic stakeholders. He lies to Rita compulsively but is sincerely convinced his motives are pure.
  • Kurt Douglas: Rita's adoptive grandfather, a Stasi-era East German border guard turned American retiree who trained Rita in police-state tradecraft from childhood. Motivations: protect Rita, and ultimately reactivate the Wolf Orchestra — the GDR sleeper ring Markus Wolf planted in America — to run a rescue channel the US state cannot see.
  • Dr. Eileen Scranton: deputy assistant to the Secretary of State for Homeland Security and Smith's boss, two levels up the reporting chain. Motivations: keep the BLACK RAIN operation inside the perimeter of cabinet politics, and open a deniable back-channel for negotiation by pretending to "lose" Rita.
  • Sonia Gomez: the hard-case DHS officer who distrusts Rita and the whole JAUNT BLUE project. Motivation: service to an authoritarian organizational culture she regards as security against the Clan; her friction with Rita encodes the Unit's internal suspicion of its own asset.
  • Patrick O'Neill: Rita's supervisor from Camp Graceland onward, a former FBI surveillance officer. Motivations: get Rita home alive and give her honest support inside a dishonest organization; he is the closest thing Rita has to a trustworthy senior.
  • Angie Hagen: Rita's Girl Scout-era girlfriend, now an electrical contractor in Philadelphia and the third-generation child of Wolf Orchestra illegals. Motivations: rekindle a relationship Rita abandoned, and channel Kurt's tradecraft into an independent rescue channel for Rita. Her coded letter to Kurt activates the Orchestra.
  • Sir Adam Burroughs: the "First Man," founder of the Radical Party and head of the Commonwealth, dying of terminal cancer through the book. Motivation: leave behind a strategy that locks the US into Mutually Assured Destruction and, later, a program for spreading democracy to the United States.
  • Adrian Holmes: Secretary of the Inner Party, effectively the Commonwealth's chief of internal power. Motivations: position himself for the succession by gathering leverage over the Burgesons, and ensure his faction survives Adam's death. The novel treats him as the Robespierre-in-waiting of the Commonwealth's own deep state.
  • Olga Thorold: Miriam's older friend and head of counter-espionage at the DPR, afflicted with multiple sclerosis. Motivation: defend Miriam and the Commonwealth, and handle Rita's capture in a way that opens negotiation rather than feeding the Party Secretariat's ambitions. She drives the transfer of Rita out of Transport Police custody.
  • Alice Morgan: Commonwealth Transport Police inspector who captures Rita. Motivation: do police work by the book and keep the suspect out of SCEP hands long enough for the DPR to take jurisdiction. Her charging ploy buys the hours that make contact possible.
  • Huw Hjorth: the Commonwealth's Explorer-General, Brilliana's husband, running the Maracaibo staging complex and JUGGERNAUT project. Motivation: industrialize world-walking itself — hovercraft carriers, pressure suits, and orbital systems — so the Commonwealth's mobility matches its ambitions.
  • Brilliana Hjorth: Miriam's first-sworn bodyguard turned DPR director. Motivation: manage the five thousand DRAGON'S TEETH carriers as an intelligence problem and requisition world-walkers (Hulius included) for the most dangerous projects.
  • Hulius Hjorth: a DPR major running courier missions into the United States; Rita's third cousin twice removed. Motivation: survive an increasingly surveilled hostile zone and serve the Commonwealth's intelligence needs; his near-capture by Transit Police resonates against Rita's own SWITCHYARD exposure.
  • John Frederick Hanover and the Dauphin: the exiled King in Exile heir and the French heir apparent, plotting in St. Petersburg. Motivations: turn the Commonwealth's technological rise into a dynastic emergency, marry off Elizabeth for alliance value, and wait for Adam's death to exploit the succession.
  • Dr. Jenn Lane (with Professor Schwartz): the clinic team that activates Rita's JAUNT BLUE ability. Motivation: translate a recessive carrier into an operational world-walker under court order and state supervision, with consequences they only partly understand.
  • Julie Straker: the HaptoTech booth colleague revealed as a DHS archaeologist at Camp Singularity. Motivation: show Rita the Forerunner ruins and the Gate so she understands the scale of what both sides are really facing.
  • Erasmus Burgeson: Miriam's husband and Propaganda Commissioner. Motivations: manage the Commonwealth's media narrative of its space program and stay healthy for his wife; his support stabilizes Miriam during the succession crisis.

Themes and concepts to track

  • Manufactured threat and coerced consent: recruitment by staged kidnapping, rooted phones, and jobs that cannot be refused.
  • The panopticon's blind spots: ubiquitous surveillance defeating itself; paper, handwriting, one-time pads, and stripped RFID tags as survivor tradecraft.
  • Blinkered intelligence cultures: transcript culture and leak discipline versus the loneliness and perishability of a single agent.
  • Technological catch-up as national security: the Commonwealth's washing-machines-and-semiconductors strategy; JUGGERNAUT as a superweapon that may never fly.
  • Succession without precedent: an untested constitution, a dying founder, a party secretary accumulating leverage, and royalists waiting at the periphery.
  • First contact as family trauma: state-to-state diplomacy routed through a mother-daughter meeting neither government can keep clean.
  • The engineered origin of magic: world-walking as designed wet-phase nanotechnology — a clue that both superpowers are inheriting someone else's technology.
  • Worst-case paranoia as doctrine: from three lost drones to fall-out detection to "nine thousand hydrogen bombs aimed at US cities."
  • Empire and republic as mirror images: royalist St. Petersburg and revolutionary New London both treating the other as an existential threat.

Core concepts

  • Intelligence: Rita's BLACK RAIN missions, Colonel Smith, Dr. Scranton, false ballast, inertial sensors, helmet cameras, Irongate Imports, debriefings, false transcripts, leak tells, and the Wolf Orchestra make the novel a sustained case in reconnaissance, deception, and counter-surveillance.
  • State Capacity: DHS, the Office of Special Programs, para-time machines, drones, genome scanners, federal databases, ARMBAND, and Commonwealth industrial mobilization show rival states trying to execute across worlds.
  • Speculative Governance: Parallel timelines let Stross test security states, revolutionary commonwealths, monarchy, bureaucracy, and family sovereignty under altered technological conditions.
  • Technological Change: Q-machines, JAUNT BLUE activation, Dawn One, semiconductor cooperatives, quantum-dot nanotechnology, drones, and ARMBAND show technical capability becoming institutional power rather than background gadgetry.
  • Legitimacy and Succession: Adam Burroughs's illness, Adrian Holmes's palace maneuvering, John Frederick, the Dauphin, Elizabeth Hanover, and Miriam's ministry make authority transfer a live strategic problem.
  • Chokepoints and Gateways: knotspace coordinates, the Gate at Camp Singularity, the switchyard as an entry point, and the Commonwealth's hovercraft staging platforms all control who and what moves between worlds.
  • Institutions: the Unit, OSP, FTO, DPR, MITI, SCEP, the Transport Police, the Inner Party secretariat, and the Wolf Orchestra are each a distinct organizational answer to the same para-time problem.
  • Information and Coordination: surveillance cameras, transcript archives, debrief committees, clone-wardrobe analysis, linguistics on recorded dialogue, and paper-channel communication show information as the coordination medium of both states.
  • Commercial Society: the rent-not-buy reading of Commonwealth housing, the price-index walkabout mission, and the Clan's smuggling past frame the two societies' economies as legible from infrastructure.
  • Empire and Periphery: French empire, the Gruinmarkt's destroyed kingdom, the ex-King in exile, and the colonial-forming Commonwealth periphery structure which powers can afford first-contact risk.
  • Industrial Policy: Miriam's prison-camp plan, semiconductor cooperatives, the Skills Transfer Program, electrified freight, and nuclear submarine programs are deliberate state-directed development.
  • Informal Institutions: the Wolf Orchestra, the Clan's surviving family networks, and Paulette Milan's legal-citizen espionage show nonstate structures persisting inside both security states.

Main Time Lines

Summary: The front matter fixes the four lines the novel runs on. Time line one diverged circa 200–250 BCE: no Judaism, Christianity, or Islam, a fully collapsed Roman empire, and medieval colonial kingdoms on America's eastern seaboard — home to the Gruinmarkt and the Clan, destroyed by US nuclear retaliation. Time line two is "almost identical to your time line" up to 2003, when Clan-conservative world-walkers nuked the White House, killed the President, and forced the government to reveal parallel universes. Time line three, discovered by Miriam, is the New American Commonwealth: a world where France invaded England in 1760, the Crown went into American exile, and the Revolution of 2003 overthrew the absolute monarchy. Time line four is an uninhabited ice-age world dotted with Forerunner ruins. Source anchors: time line one; Gruinmarkt; Clan; time line two; White House; time line three; New American Commonwealth; time line four; Forerunner ruins.

Main Character Profiles

Summary: Profiles fix the four principals. MIRIAM BURGESON (born 1968) is the Boston journalist who discovered the Clan, fled the Gruinmarkt, led survivors into the Commonwealth, took part in its revolution, and now warns that "the USA is coming." RITA DOUGLAS (born 1995) is an adopted, history-and-drama-student actress who has learned to keep her head down in President Rumsfeld's post-7/16 surveillance America — and who has no idea the government has "big plans" for her. Source anchors: Eric Smith; Kurt Douglas; Miriam Burgeson; Rita Douglas; Greta Douglas.

Analysis: The profiles authorize what the novel will dramatize: individuals the size of Institutions. Smith, Kurt, and Miriam are each the human face of an organizational logic, while Rita is the raw recruit that both systems want to metabolize. Kurt's profile plants the Intelligence theme of trained evasion — he has prepared Rita "for living in a police state" — and Miriam's Profile makes the seventeen-year tech race State Capacity in miniature. PART ONE — DOG AND PONY SHOW

Hulius Hjorth, Fort George protocols, and Paulette Milan's antibiotic research make Technological Change a political mechanism, because clandestinely imported knowledge becomes medicine, factories, and ministries inside the Commonwealth. Watertown Semiconductor and Jeffrey's shooting prove Miriam's modernization program has made her a royalist target. Iris's rebuttal is the most explicitly theoretical passage in the book — give Adam "political levers, not shiny scientific toys" — which is Industrial Policy understood as persuasion before production. Paulette's deliberation about extraction frames Commercial Society from the agent's side: the Commonwealth is obligating loyalty that time line two materially already paid for.

Empire Games

PART TWO — FAST TRACK

In Phoenix, Rita tells Emily about her DHS job, learns that Iris Beckstein arranged the adoption through Kurt, and then drinks beer with Kurt while he explains Stasi informers, false names, Mrs. Beckstein, Greta, and why secret police always lie. Source anchors: Camp Graceland, Patrick O'Neill, Anna Mittal, Emily Douglas, Iris Beckstein, Kurt Douglas, The Grasshopper Lies Heavy, one-time pad.

Analysis: Camp Graceland and Anna Mittal show that DHS is trying to split Rita into operational selves before she understands her own family history. Emily Douglas and Iris Beckstein make the adoption a chain of protective choices, while Kurt Douglas refuses the federal version of world-walker guilt. The Grasshopper Lies Heavy and the one-time pad give Rita a counter-channel that neither Patrick O'Neill nor Gomez controls. Kurt's offer of a PKD novel whose annotations hide a code is Intelligence as pure tradecraft, and his admission that he "changed his name" dissents from the legal category of identity the security state administers.

Surgical Intervention

Summary: Dr. Jenn Lane explains world-walking to Rita through Q-machines, quantum dots, lateral occipital trigger engrams, recessive glutamate receptors, JAUNT BLUE, and a short interfering RNA infusion into cerebrospinal fluid. Analysis: Dr. Jenn Lane makes JAUNT BLUE bodily and invasive: Q-machines, trigger engrams, and lumbar puncture turn Rita's world-walking inheritance into a medical procedure under court order. The left forearm implant is a state-owned replacement for HaptoTech's harmless demo implants, changing her from contractor to weapon. Marianne and Scientology show how Camp Graceland mixes surveillance medicine with private belief, while Kurt's hidden knot proves DHS is not the only party that understands the mechanism. The FISA-court oath means the state converts its own law into a license to commit a felony on its business — Institutions absorbing the ability rather than merely inheriting it — and the whole procedure is a demonstration of Technological Change as human-machine integration.

False ballast, inertial sensors, helmet cameras, Irongate Imports, broad track gauge, blue signals, and electric freight make Intelligence cumulative: Rita's reconnaissance turns small infrastructural details into Smith's first hard assessment that BLACK RAIN is not primitive. Rita's body opens the door, but the plan still depends on distributed devices. The gauge measurement — five-foot-one rather than standard — and the blue signals instead of red/yellow/green are the chapter's quiet evidence of Path Dependence and Institutional Drift: a world whose railroads industrialized separately from ours.

Switches

Adam chooses MAD as a communication protocol as much as a threat. Eugene, electric multiple units, and sixty tanks prove that Rita has walked into a militarized industrial society, not a rail museum, while one station mate is enough to connect American HUMINT to Commonwealth counterintelligence. The air-cushion Redoubt with doppelganger bunkers in empty timelines demonstrates State Capacity deployed on the same logic as the Americans' own staging platforms: continuity of government as an engineered, world-crossing default.
