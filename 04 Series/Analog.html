Analog

Series problem

The locally represented Analog novels ask what political autonomy means when attention, identity, and public choice are mediated by privately controlled feeds. The series begins with manipulation hidden inside ordinary platform economics and then widens toward the infrastructures and cross-border networks through which institutions can resist or reproduce that control.

Represented local volumes

  • Bandwidth
  • Borderless

The third volume, Breach, is not locally represented. This hub therefore stops at the institutional position reached by Borderless and makes no claims about the missing volume.

Series-scale movement

Bandwidth establishes the feed as both an intimate interface and a governance system: firms can allocate visibility, steer political salience, and convert behavioral knowledge into strategic leverage. Borderless shifts the emphasis from discovering that power to organizing around it. The problem becomes how technical operators, firms, political coalitions, and informal networks coordinate across jurisdictions when the informational substrate is itself contested.

That movement changes the meaning of agency. Individual disclosure or refusal is insufficient once influence is embedded in standards, capital, infrastructure, and routine use. The represented sequence therefore moves from the ethics of manipulation toward the design of countervailing institutions.
