Orthogonal Trilogy

Core series problem

This trilogy asks what kinds of civilization, politics, and scientific development emerge when the underlying physics of the world differ radically from ours.

Why the series matters in this vault

It matters because it is one of the strongest reminders that institutions are downstream from substrate. Many political theories quietly assume our world's energetic, informational, and physical conditions. Orthogonal breaks those assumptions open and rebuilds society from altered first principles.

That makes the trilogy especially valuable for the vault's deeper ambition: not just to compare governments, but to compare the constraints that make particular governments thinkable.

Main recurring tensions

  • Scientific understanding versus inherited assumptions.
  • Civilizational survival versus substrate-imposed limit.
  • Knowledge preservation versus discovery under urgent threat.
  • Institutional design under radically altered physical constraint.

Volume-by-volume arc

  • The Clockwork Rocket: first principles of the world force science and social order to reconstitute each other.
  • The Eternal Flame: not currently represented by a local child volume note. Treat references to this middle volume as series-context only until a source-backed note exists.
  • The Arrows of Time: civilization-scale response becomes inseparable from the physical structure of the world itself.

Local coverage

The local vault currently has source-backed notes for The Clockwork Rocket and The Arrows of Time. It also has the older hub note Orthogonal Trilogy. The middle volume, The Eternal Flame, should not be treated as a completed local book note until an extracted source and child note are present.

What the series keeps teaching

The trilogy keeps returning to a foundational lesson for the whole vault: institutions are not free-floating. They inherit the physics, ecology, and information structure of their world. Change the substrate and you change the menu of viable politics.

Why this matters beyond plot

Orthogonal matters because it disciplines political imagination. It prevents the reader from smuggling human institutions in as universal defaults. That makes it a particularly sharp partner to the vault's ecological, technological, and speculative-governance notes.
